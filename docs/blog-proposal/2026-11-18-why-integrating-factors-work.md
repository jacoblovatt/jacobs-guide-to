An integrating factor can look like a trick: multiply a differential equation
by an exponential, integrate, and somehow the answer appears. The part that
makes the method worth remembering is the reason for that multiplier.

I have written a chapter that starts with the product rule and asks what would
make the coefficients match. It follows that idea through a worked example,
checks the answer by substitution and ends with two exercises and solutions.
It also explains where domain restrictions and the arbitrary constant enter.

[Read “Why integrating factors work” in Jacob’s Guide to Calculus](https://jacoblovatt.github.io/jacobs-guide-to/calculus/why-integrating-factors-work.html).

This is the first complete chapter in a collection of living guides developed
from my learning. The aim is to build explanations carefully, one question at
a time, with HTML and PDF versions from the same source.
